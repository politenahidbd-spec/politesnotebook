I've kept a notebook, in some form, for almost twenty years. Small ones, mostly. The kind that fit in a coat pocket and get bent at the corners.

## Why bother

Because memory is a bad photographer. It crops the wrong way, it loses the light, it makes you the subject of every picture. A notebook is more honest. It writes down what actually happened, even when what actually happened was nothing.

## What I look for

A shape. A gesture. A sentence I overhear on the tram. I don't try to be clever. Cleverness fades. What I want is the small, exact fact — the colour of the sky over the bridge at 7:12, the fact that the woman across from me was reading a book with a red cover and crying quietly.

Later, when I sit down to write or to make a photograph or a film, those small facts are the things I return to. They are the raw material. Everything else — the meaning, the story, the argument — comes second.

## A rule I try to keep

Write it down before you know what it means.

If I wait until I understand something, I lose the version of it that was worth remembering.
